PORTAL: THE FRAMEWORK NOW ASKS THE REQUEST'S EXPERIENCE, NOT THE STAFF SESSION
Date: 2026-08-09 (corrected + extended 2026-08-10)
Category: 2 (framework-core behavior change - audit your own code)

SUMMARY
    The close-out sweep of the identity/session/polymorphic epic audited every
    Session:: call site in the framework and the template app against the final
    one-table session architecture. It found places where framework code asked
    the STAFF session facade a question that belongs to whichever experience is
    actually being served, and fixed them - first seven call sites (upload,
    preview, attachment claim, mail, realtime x3), then, in the correction batch
    that follows it, THE TENANT BOUNDARY ITSELF plus the remaining site seams,
    and experience scoping for flash alerts.

    You get the fixes automatically. What you may have to do by hand is AUDIT
    YOUR OWN CODE, because several of them change what your handlers receive or
    what your data is scoped to - silently, with no error and no failed build.
    If you have compensating workarounds for the old behavior, this is where you
    find and remove them.

    Full evidence, per-file verdict tables and the findings that were RECORDED
    rather than fixed:
        docs.dev/audits/portal_realm_session_audit_2026_08_09.md   (in the
        framework monorepo; the summary below is self-contained)

THE ONE RULE BEHIND ALL SEVEN
    Fork on the EXPERIENCE OF THE REQUEST:

        App\RSpade\Core\Portal\Rsx_Portal::is_portal_request()

    NEVER on who happens to be signed in:

        Portal_Session::is_logged_in()      <-- WRONG as an experience test

    "A portal user is logged in" is an IDENTITY test and it gets both directions
    wrong - and MORE wrong now that a browser has ONE session carrying both
    identities at once (see upstream_changes/session_table_merge_08_09.txt). An
    anonymous portal caller (a public portal page, your invite or register flow, a
    portal tab that logged out) falls into the STAFF branch and is handed the
    staff user, the staff tenant and staff-shaped authorization on a portal page.
    A staff caller whose browser is also signed into the portal falls into the
    PORTAL branch and gets the portal tenant on a staff page. Both are
    data-exposure shapes, not cosmetic ones.

    On the previous two-cookie design this misrouting could also reach
    Session::get_session_id() and MINT a second session, setting a staff cookie on
    a portal response. That specific failure is structurally impossible now (one
    cookie, one row), but the identity-vs-experience confusion behind it is not -
    which is why the rule still stands.

    If you have written this predicate anywhere in your own app, fix it there
    too. Grep for it:

        grep -rn "Portal_Session::is_logged_in()" rsx/ | grep -v "rsx/portal/"

    A hit INSIDE your portal tree is usually fine (there the realm is not in
    question). A hit in shared code, an event handler, a service, or a model is
    the shape to check.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ACTION REQUIRED - audit these four, in this order
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. YOUR file.upload.authorize HANDLER  (highest value - it is mandatory, so you
   have one)

    WHAT CHANGED
        /_upload builds its gate payload and derives site_id by REALM now, not
        by portal-login state. Two behaviors changed:

          - An ANONYMOUS upload on a portal request now reports
            'user' => null (the portal user) and resolves site_id from
            Portal_Session. It used to report the STAFF user and the staff site.
            Note it previously answered HTTP 400 "Unable to resolve a site for
            this upload" whenever the staff site was 0 - if you worked around
            that by requiring login on public portal upload forms, the
            workaround is no longer needed.

          - A STAFF upload made from a browser that ALSO holds a portal cookie
            now correctly reports the staff user and the staff site. It used to
            report the PORTAL user and stamp the attachment with the PORTAL's
            site.

    WHAT TO DO
        Open your handler (usually rsx/handlers/File_Upload_Handlers.php) and
        confirm it still says what you mean. Specifically:

          - If it type-checks the 'user' key (instanceof User_Model, a role
            lookup, ->role_id), make sure a Portal_User_Model or null arriving
            on a portal request produces the answer you intend rather than an
            accidental deny-everything or allow-everything.
          - If it compensates for the old crossover ("if a portal user is
            present, treat it as a portal upload"), DELETE that - the framework
            now hands you a realm-honest payload and your compensation will
            fight it.

        The framework's own template handler is the worked example; it uses a
        deliberate staff-OR-portal disjunction and needed no change.

2. YOUR file.thumbnail.authorize HANDLER

    WHAT CHANGED
        File_Preview_Controller::get_preview_info is #[Auth_Realm('any')], so a
        portal page's <Document_Preview> reaches it as a genuine PORTAL request.
        Its gate payload's 'user' key used to be a staff-facade read - null on a
        portal domain, or THE STAFF USER in prefix mode, where the same browser is
        usually signed into the staff app too. It is now experience-honest.

    WHY THIS ONE MATTERS MOST
        If your handler authorizes previews by inspecting $data['user'], you
        were granting portal viewers access on the strength of a staff user's
        permissions whenever a staff session happened to be present in the same
        browser. Re-read the handler with that in mind, then re-test a portal
        preview while NOT logged in as staff in the same browser - that is the
        case that used to behave differently and now does not.

3. ANY CODE THAT CLAIMS AN UPLOAD FROM A PORTAL ENDPOINT

    WHAT CHANGED
        File_Attachment_Model::can_user_assign_this_file() compares the
        attachment's site against the CURRENT EXPERIENCE's site now. /_upload
        always stamped a portal upload with the portal site, so the claim check
        was asking a different facade than the stamp came from and could only
        succeed when the staff identity happened to be on the same tenant.

    WHAT TO DO
        If you have a portal endpoint that claims an uploaded file (attach_to /
        add_to after find_by_key), re-test it. If you added a workaround -
        setting the staff site before claiming, or skipping
        can_user_assign_this_file() because it "never passes in the portal" -
        remove it. Claims that legitimately failed before will now succeed,
        which is the point, but it does mean a code path you may have treated as
        dead is live again.

4. PORTAL-TRIGGERED EMAIL

    WHAT CHANGED
        Rsx_Mail::send() and the blocklist API (is_blocked / block / unblock /
        block_all) resolve the site by realm. A send from a portal request is
        now filed under the PORTAL's site.

    WHY IT MATTERS
        That site does two things: it stamps the _email_queue row, and it drives
        the Email_Recipient_Model::is_blocked($site_id, ...) unsubscribe lookup.
        Under the old behavior a portal-triggered NOTIFICATION or MARKETING
        email consulted the wrong site's blocklist - so a recipient who had
        unsubscribed could still be mailed. TRANSACTIONAL sends were unaffected
        (they always deliver).

    WHAT TO DO
        - If your app reads _email_queue rows filtered by site (an admin screen,
          a report, a metric), portal-originated mail now appears under a
          different site_id than before. Historical rows are NOT rewritten.
        - If you called Rsx_Mail::block()/unblock() from portal code, the
          blocklist entries it wrote went to the wrong site. Check
          _email_recipients for rows whose site_id looks wrong for the address,
          and re-create them under the correct site if a user's unsubscribe was
          silently ineffective.
        - CLI is not a portal request, so your queue drain and any console
          sending are unchanged.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NO ACTION - fixed, listed so you can recognize the behavior change
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5. Realtime::connection_token()
        Picked the facade by portal-login state, so an unauthenticated portal page
        was stamped as a staff connection (and, on the old two-cookie design,
        minted a staff session opening a socket). It now forks by EXPERIENCE for
        the identity and the site, while session_id is resolved once - one browser,
        one session - and gated on has_session() so opening a socket never creates
        a session as a side effect. The token's session_id may be null where it
        previously was a freshly-minted id; null was already part of the documented
        token contract, and only the Node relay reads it. The connection 'realm'
        stamp survives with a precise meaning: which EXPERIENCE the page was minted
        on, so a portal-property change refreshes portal tabs and leaves staff tabs
        on the same session alone.

6. Realtime::_current_site_id()
        Same predicate, same fix - and note the two tenants are now genuinely
        different columns (site_id is staff, portal_site_id is portal). A
        not-signed-in portal visitor subscribing to a public topic was handed the
        staff site, and the relay's site-match then rejected every subscribe. If you have a public (unauthenticated)
        portal surface with a realtime subscription that "just never worked",
        try it again.

7. Realtime_Emissions::_resolve_site_id()
        The fallback used when a $realtime model has NO site_id column of its
        own. On a portal request it used the staff session's site; it now uses
        the portal's. Frames for such models were being routed to the wrong
        tenant's connections on a multi-tenant install.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SITE SEAMS - THE TENANT BOUNDARY IS REALM-AWARE NOW (2026-08-10)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    An earlier revision of this document told you NOT to touch your
    Main::init() site declaration, because the ORM's tenant boundary still read
    the staff facade and that line was accidentally holding the portal up. THAT
    IS NOW FIXED. Read this section instead; it supersedes that instruction.

8. Rsx_Site_Model_Abstract::get_current_site_id()   (the big one)

    WHAT CHANGED
        The tenant boundary for EVERY site-scoped model - it feeds the site
        global scope, the forced site_id on create, the cross-site save and
        retrieve fatals, save(), insert, and the site write lock - now asks
        Rsx_Portal::is_portal_request() first:

            portal request -> Portal_Session::get_site_id()
                              (the site YOUR app declared for the portal;
                               THROWS if it declared none)
            otherwise      -> Session::get_site_id()   (unchanged)

        Before this, a portal request was scoped by whatever tenant the
        co-resident STAFF cookie happened to be on (prefix mode - correct only
        by coincidence on a mono-site install, a cross-tenant read/write
        primitive on a multi-tenant one), or by site 0 on a portal DOMAIN, where
        the portal simply could not work.

    ACTION REQUIRED - three things, in this order

    a) MAKE SURE YOU DECLARE THE PORTAL SITE. If you have not adopted
       Portal_Session::set_site_id() (normally in Portal_Main::init()), your
       portal now throws on its first site-scoped query, with a message naming
       the call and `php artisan rsx:man portal`. That refusal is deliberate: a
       portal that does not know its tenant must not pick one.

    b) YOUR Main::init() SITE DECLARATION IS NOW STAFF-ONLY IN EFFECT, and you
       may treat it as such. Nothing portal rides on it any more. You do NOT
       have to change it - a plain `Session::set_site_id($n)` there is still the
       right way to declare the STAFF app's tenancy (and the CLI default) - but
       the comment we shipped telling you it was load-bearing for the portal is
       obsolete, and guarding it with is_portal_request() no longer breaks
       portal login. We proved that by neutralizing the line entirely and
       running a full portal form login plus site-scoped portal Ajax.

    c) REMOVE PORTAL-SIDE COMPENSATION. Grep your portal tree for code that was
       working around the old behavior:

           grep -rn "Session::set_site_id" rsx/portal/ rsx/
           grep -rn "without_site_scope\|withoutGlobalScope" rsx/portal/
           grep -rn "where('site_id'" rsx/portal/

       Any staff-facade site write made from portal code to "fix" scoping, any
       manual site_id predicate added because the scope was wrong, and any
       without_site_scope() escape hatch opened for that reason should go. They
       now FIGHT the correct scope: a hand-written where('site_id', ...) on top
       of a correctly-scoped query is at best redundant and at worst an
       impossible predicate returning nothing.

9. Rsx_Time, Rsx_Sms, Rsx_Settings, Rsx_Throttle

    WHAT CHANGED
        The same fork, at the four remaining site seams:

        Rsx_Time      - get_site_timezone() and the site tier of
                        get_user_timezone() resolve the PORTAL's site on a
                        portal request. And there is no user tier there:
                        portal_users has no timezone column, so a portal page
                        renders in the SITE's zone. It used to fall through to
                        config('rsx.datetime.default_timezone') on a portal
                        domain, or - worse, in prefix mode - render a client
                        portal page in the STAFF user's personal timezone.
                        The memo is now keyed on the experience too.

        Rsx_Sms       - site stamps the queue row and drives the
                        Sms_Recipient_Model::is_blocked() opt-out lookup;
                        identical to the Rsx_Mail change in item 4 above.

        Rsx_Settings  - a site-scoped setting read or written on a portal
                        request lands under the portal's site. Passing an
                        explicit $site_id still wins, unchanged.

        Rsx_Throttle  - the (site_id, user_id, action_key) bucket is keyed on
                        the portal's site. NOTE the identity half is still a
                        LOGIN user id by contract; if you write the first portal
                        throttle (a portal login rate limit is the obvious one),
                        you decide how to keep portal_users.id out of the staff
                        id space - a realm-qualified action_key is the cheapest
                        answer.

    WHAT TO DO
        - If you display timestamps in your portal, re-check them: they now
          follow the portal site's timezone. If you compensated by converting
          zones by hand in portal templates or controllers, remove it.
        - If you send SMS from portal code, re-test the opt-out path and check
          _sms_recipients for rows filed under the wrong site (same remedy as
          the email blocklist in item 4).
        - If you adopted Rsx_Settings, re-check any site-scoped key your portal
          reads or writes; values written from portal code before this release
          are under the STAFF site and are not migrated.

10. FLASH ALERTS ARE EXPERIENCE-SCOPED (_flash_alerts.is_portal)

    WHAT CHANGED
        A new column, is_portal, is stamped from Rsx_Portal::is_portal_request()
        when an alert is queued and filtered when it is read. One browser has
        one session shared by both experiences, so before this an alert queued
        by a portal page was delivered to whichever page of that browser
        rendered NEXT - including a staff admin screen showing "Welcome to the
        Client Portal". The per-session cap is now per (session_id, is_portal),
        so a runaway queue in one experience cannot evict the other's messages.
        The migration ships with the release; existing rows default to staff and
        live at most a minute anyway.

    ACTION REQUIRED
        Only if you query _flash_alerts directly. The API (Flash_Alert::success
        / error / info / warning, get_pending_messages) is unchanged and does
        the right thing on its own.

            grep -rn "_flash_alerts" rsx/

        A direct read must now carry the experience predicate as well as the
        session id, or it will consume the other experience's messages:

            ->where('session_id', $session_id)
            ->where('is_portal', $is_portal)      // <-- add this

        A direct WRITE (do not do this - use Flash_Alert) must stamp it.

11. STILL OUTSTANDING - the file-SERVING routes

    /_download/:key, /_inline/:key, /_download_zip/:key, /_thumbnail/*,
    /_icon_by_extension/:extension, /_preview/pdf/:key and /_preview/pdfjs.mjs
    carry #[Route] only - no #[Portal_Route], unlike /_upload. In prefix mode a
    portal page's <img src="/_inline/..."> or PDF fetch therefore lands on the
    STAFF channel, and your file.download.authorize handler sees a staff user
    (or null) for a file a PORTAL user is viewing. On a portal DOMAIN they 404
    outright, so portal images, downloads and PDF previews do not load at all.

    This is an architectural decision (extend the whole file-serving family the
    way /_upload was done), not a facade bug, and it is deliberately not changed
    here. If you are planning a portal DOMAIN deployment, raise it before you
    build against it - that topology has still never been exercised end to end.
